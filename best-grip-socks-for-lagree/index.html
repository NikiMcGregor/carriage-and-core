Lagree is taught on the Megaformer, a machine its creator says is distinct from a Pilates reformer. One practical difference for your feet: the Megaformer has standing platforms at both ends, and classes move between them while keeping muscles under tension. That puts more weight-bearing time on your soles than a typical reformer class, and it means quick foot repositioning between exercises.

So for Lagree we weight the grip-sock decision differently than for Pilates. We have not tested these socks ourselves; the reasoning below combines the machine's design (from the sources listed) with each sock's published specs.

What changes for Lagree

  1. Hold beats feel. A sock that rotates a few degrees during a long lunge on the platform is distracting. Arch bands and higher cuts help keep the sole aligned.
  2. Full-sole grip. Standing work loads the heel and the ball of the foot. Look for grip that covers both.
  3. Quick on, quick off. Some studios ask you to remove shoes and socks at the door; others don't. Closed-toe socks go on fastest when you're late.
  4. Spares. A sweaty, sustained class can leave socks damp. If you take back-to-back classes, a second pair matters more than a premium pair.

Questions readers ask

Can I use my Pilates grip socks for Lagree?

Usually yes — most grip socks are marketed for Pilates, barre and similar classes. The question is whether your current pair stays put through long holds and quick moves between the front and back platforms.

Do Lagree studios require grip socks?

Many do. Policies vary by studio, so check the booking page or ask the front desk.
